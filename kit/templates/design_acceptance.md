# Design acceptance: ID and surface

*For a task that changes what a customer sees: a page, a screen, an email, a widget. The task's SPEC or fast brief names the surfaces and the brand kit in its Design acceptance section. The implementer fills this file in the task's evidence folder (`program/evidence/<ID>/design.md`) before handing back, viewing every screenshot; QA views every screenshot again and writes its own verdict. Passing tests do not show a page that reads as a plain document where a designed page was meant, or a theme that drifted from the brand: only looking does.*

Brand kit: *the path of the brand kit (logo and wordmark files, palette tokens, type, minimum sizes, clear space)*

Widths: *desktop 1440, tablet 768 and phone 390 pixels, unless the brief names others*

## Surfaces

*One row per surface, width and theme, light and dark: every combination. Where the surface existed before, a before and an after screenshot. Screenshots are files the reviewer can open (in the task's evidence folder or the report's folder), never only a description.*

## Brand checks

- Logo and wordmark: the brand kit's own files, at or above their minimum size, with their clear space; never redrawn as text in another typeface.
- Palette: colours from the brand kit's tokens, each in the role the kit gives it (text, surface, accent); an accent the kit allows only on dark grounds never appears as text on light ones.
- Type: the brand's families and weights.
- Layout: the page is designed, not a plain document: the structure the brief names (for a landing page a hero, calls to action and the media the copy refers to); nothing overflows, overlaps or is cut off at any width.
- Contrast: text and controls stay readable in both themes.
- Words: the customer-facing names the program's rulings require (no internal or third-party product name where the customer should see the program's own).

## Verdict

*Implementer: what was compared with the brand kit, and anything left open. QA: accept, or numbered findings, each naming the screenshot that shows it.*
